
## 🟢 OPTIONAL — Wikipedia citation as source (~40 min, slow-compound)

### WHAT
Find one Wikipedia article where SculptClub could be a credible primary source for a factual claim, add a `<ref>` citation pointing to a SculptClub URL. Target: Wikipedia article on "Personal training", "Jordaan (Amsterdam neighborhood)", or "Boutique gym".

### WHY
Wikipedia citation = **+75 Distribution Oracle multiplier** for slow-compound, durable visibility. Wikipedia citations stay indexed forever, drive ~50-200 page-views per month for a niche article + serve as a credibility signal in search results AND in LLM training data. **High effort to land but indefinite payoff.**

**Cost of skipping:** zero LLM citation likelihood. 0 Wikipedia referrals. No "trusted source" signal in AI Overview / LLM responses.

### TIME
~40 min total: 15 min finding the right article + suitable claim, 10 min writing the citation in Wikipedia format, 15 min reading their Conflict of Interest policy + verifying you can do this without violating it.

### HOW
1. **Read Wikipedia's COI policy first** (15 min — non-negotiable):
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Conflict_of_interest
   Key rule: **DO NOT create a SculptClub article**. DO NOT add SculptClub to "see also" links. Only valid use: SculptClub URL is a **citation source for a specific factual claim** that is verifiable on the SculptClub site.

2. **Find an article that needs a citation for a fact you can actually verify**:
   - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personal_trainer (look for [citation needed] tags on facts about pricing, rental models, freelancer dynamics)
   - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordaan (look for facts about businesses, services in the neighborhood)
   - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boutique_gym (this article is short — could expand with a real example)

3. **Pick ONE specific factual claim** that:
   - Is currently uncited or has [citation needed]
   - Can be supported by content on the SculptClub site (e.g., "freelance personal trainers in some private studios pay rent rather than commission" — supported by `/nl/studio-huren` page content)
   - Is NOT promotional ("SculptClub is the best gym in Amsterdam" = NEVER OK)

4. **Add the citation** using Wikipedia's `<ref>` syntax:
   ```
   <ref>{{cite web |url=https://sculptclub.nl/en/studio-rental |title=Studio rental for freelance personal trainers |publisher=SculptClub |access-date=2026-05-07}}</ref>
   ```

5. **Edit the article** — add the citation inline supporting the existing factual claim. Save with edit summary like "Added citation for freelance PT studio rental model".

6. **Watch for revert** — Wikipedia editors check new edits. Most reverts happen within 7 days. If reverted, READ the reason (usually "not a reliable source" or "promotional"). DO NOT edit-war. Take the L.

### VERIFY
After 14 days:
- View the article history: edit should still be present (not reverted)
- Check Plausible Sources tab: `wikipedia.org` or `wikimedia.org` referrals appearing
- The compound: 30-90 days later, run a Google search for the claim — your SculptClub URL should rank in the page-1 citations

### IF STUCK
- **All articles I find don't need citations:** that's the most common outcome. Wikipedia is dense; most articles are well-cited. Don't force it. Skip and revisit in 6 months.
- **My edit got reverted:** read the reason, don't argue. Common reverts: "non-notable source", "promotional", "primary source preferred". You may not be the right source for this article. Move on.
- **Account too new:** Wikipedia edits from accounts <4 days old + <10 prior edits often get auto-reverted. Edit a few unrelated articles first (typo fixes, dead-link removal) to build edit history.
- **Cannot understand the syntax:** use Wikipedia's visual editor (toggle in top-right of edit page). Click "Cite" → pick "Web" template → fill in URL/title/publisher. Save.
